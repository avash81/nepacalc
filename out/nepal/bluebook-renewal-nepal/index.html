Quick Answer

In Nepal, the Bluebook is the common name for a vehicle's registration certificate. Renewing it means completing the applicable requirements to keep that certificate valid. The total amount and process depend on the vehicle, the province, the renewal status and any other requirements such as insurance. There is no single nationwide vehicle-tax rate or renewal fee. Vehicle taxation is administered through each province's legal framework, while national law provides the registration and renewal structure. The applicable government authority determines the final legally correct amount.

What Is a Bluebook?

The Bluebook is the commonly used name for a vehicle's registration certificate. An official Department of Transport Management resource describes it as a certificate containing the description of the vehicle.

The Bluebook is not the same as:

  • a driver's licence (which belongs to the driver, not the vehicle);
  • vehicle insurance;
  • vehicle tax or road tax;
  • a route permit (for applicable public or hired vehicles);
  • a pollution or emissions certificate; or
  • an ownership-transfer document.

These are connected during vehicle administration and renewal, but each is a separate document or obligation.

What Does Bluebook Renewal Mean?

Bluebook renewal means renewing the validity of the vehicle registration certificate after it expires. Under the national Motor Vehicles and Transport Management Act, registration certificates must be renewed through the prescribed application and payment process. Section 31 of the Act addresses registration-certificate renewal, while Sections 33–35 address multi-year renewal, cancellation and re-registration.

The practical process differs between provinces because vehicle tax and transport-service administration operate within each province's own legal framework. An instruction, tax amount or online procedure verified for one province may not apply to another.

Bluebook Renewal vs Vehicle Tax

This distinction is a frequent source of confusion. Bluebook renewal and vehicle tax are not the same thing, although they are often handled during the same transaction.

Vehicle tax

Vehicle tax is the recurring tax applicable to a registered vehicle under the relevant provincial law. Depending on the vehicle and province, the tax schedule can be based on engine capacity, motor power, seating capacity, carrying capacity, vehicle type or another applicable specification.

Renewal fee

This is the charge associated with renewing the registration certificate itself, separate from the vehicle-tax amount. Check the applicable government source for the current renewal-fee schedule.

Insurance

The Motor Vehicles and Transport Management Act provides that required insurance must be obtained and that a registration certificate will not be renewed without it. Insurance is therefore a legal prerequisite, not an optional cost. The Nepal Insurance Authority is the current regulatory body for vehicle insurance in Nepal and publishes relevant insurance circulars. Insurance premiums are separate from the provincial vehicle-tax amount.

Pollution certification

The Motor Vehicles and Transport Management Rules include pollution-related information for applicable vehicles within the renewal-documentation framework. Where applicable rules require a pollution certificate, this must be available for renewal. Requirements depend on vehicle type, age, fuel type and current transport-office procedure.

Route permit

A route permit is a separate transport authorisation for public or hired vehicles. It is not a Bluebook. Renewing a Bluebook does not renew a route permit. The Transport Management Rules include route-permit information for applicable vehicles within the renewal-documentation framework.

Who Sets Vehicle Tax in Nepal?

Vehicle tax is administered within Nepal's seven provinces, each of which has its own applicable provincial economic legislation and transport-service framework. The Department of Transport Management provides transport-office references, tax-rate circulars and provincial economic-law references including separate legal schedules for each province.

This means that asking “how much is vehicle tax in Nepal?” without specifying the province and vehicle category is not a complete question. The correct question is:

What is the current vehicle tax and renewal requirement for my vehicle under the applicable province and vehicle category?

A rate published for Bagmati Province should not automatically be presented as the rate for all of Nepal. The existing NepaCalc Vehicle Tax Calculator is explicitly Bagmati-based for its current calculation tables.

What Changes by Province?

Provincial differences may affect:

  • vehicle-tax rates and the applicable category classification;
  • additional provincial charges beyond the basic tax;
  • online service availability and the extent of digital renewal;
  • payment workflows and accepted payment methods;
  • any concessions, waivers or special notices in effect; and
  • transport-office procedures and processing times.

What does not change by province:

  • the basic concept of vehicle registration and renewal;
  • the national legal framework (Motor Vehicles and Transport Management Act);
  • the distinction between a Bluebook and a driving licence; and
  • the requirement to comply with applicable law regardless of province.

What Changes by Vehicle?

Vehicle category substantially affects the applicable tax and requirements:

Motorcycle / scooter

Engine capacity (CC) is typically the primary classification factor for provincial tax.

Private car / jeep

Vehicle type, engine capacity or another applicable provincial classification determines the tax.

Electric vehicle

EV annual road tax and EV import taxation are completely different obligations. Provincial EV tax classifications may differ.

Public / commercial

Route permits, passenger or load capacity, and additional transport requirements apply. Not comparable to private-vehicle renewal.

When Should You Renew?

The safest approach is to renew before the registration certificate expires. The national Motor Vehicles and Transport Management Act provides a period after expiry within which renewal can still proceed before delayed-renewal provisions apply. However, this should not be read as a recommendation to wait until after expiry. Renewing before expiry avoids:

  • late charges and penalties;
  • additional paperwork and documentation requirements;
  • service delays at transport offices;
  • potential insurance complications; and
  • uncertainty about the exact amount due.

What Happens When Renewal Is Late?

This area requires careful distinction between two separate legal frameworks.

National registration-renewal law

The Motor Vehicles and Transport Management Act provides a period after a certificate's stated validity for renewal before delayed-renewal provisions apply. The Act specifies escalating additional charges for delayed renewal depending on the length of delay, and contains provisions for eventual cancellation and re-registration where a vehicle remains unrenewed for an extended period.

Provincial vehicle-tax late-payment rules

Provincial economic legislation and transport rules may separately specify the treatment of late vehicle-tax payments. These are different from the national registration-renewal law. A calculator that describes a specific province's late-payment penalty is describing that province's tax law, not necessarily a nationwide rule.

Long-overdue or cancelled registrations

There is an important legal difference between a registration that is slightly overdue and one that has remained unrenewed long enough to reach statutory cancellation. A vehicle owner should not assume that a long-overdue vehicle can simply be renewed by multiplying one year's tax by the number of missed years. The Act contains distinct provisions for cancellation and re-registration. Owners of long-overdue vehicles should contact the relevant transport authority rather than relying on a generic online estimate.

Documents and Requirements

Rule 8 of the Motor Vehicles and Transport Management Rules addresses renewal of the registration certificate, and Schedule 11 of the Rules specifies the information submitted with a renewal application, including vehicle registration details, owner information, expiry, tax evidence, route-permit details for hired vehicles, and pollution information where applicable.

Requirements vary by vehicle, transaction and current office procedure. Commonly relevant items include:

  • original Bluebook/registration certificate;
  • vehicle-tax payment evidence;
  • valid required insurance certificate;
  • pollution certificate where applicable;
  • route permit for applicable public or hired vehicles;
  • ownership or identification documents where required;
  • payment receipts; and
  • any other documents specified by the relevant transport office.
The checklist above reflects the general legal framework. Always check the current notice from the relevant transport authority before visiting. Office requirements and procedures can change.

Insurance and Pollution Requirements

Insurance

The Motor Vehicles and Transport Management Act provides that required insurance must be obtained and that registration renewal cannot proceed without it. The Nepal Insurance Authority is the current regulatory body and publishes applicable insurance circulars including third-party vehicle insurance requirements. Insurance premiums are separate from provincial vehicle tax and from the renewal fee. Do not publish fixed insurance amounts without verifying against a current Nepal Insurance Authority circular or applicable insurer schedule.

Pollution certification

Where the applicable rules require pollution testing or certification, the relevant certificate must be available for renewal. Requirements depend on vehicle type, age, fuel type and current transport-office procedure. Check the current requirements from the relevant transport authority.

Online Vehicle Tax and Renewal

Online vehicle-registration and tax-payment systems are being deployed through provincial government systems, but the services available and the extent to which a transaction can be completed online vary by province and may change.

Do not assume that online tax payment automatically means the entire Bluebook-renewal process is complete. Some provinces currently provide full digital workflows; others have systems where tax payment can be initiated online but certain steps still require transport-office processing.

As of this guide's last review (6 September 2026), the Sudurpashchim provincial VRS page marks vehicle registration and tax payment as “Coming Soon.” The Lumbini provincial VRS page similarly shows some functions as still being introduced. Both portals exist at the entry level but detailed service availability is not yet uniform.

Always check the applicable provincial portal directly and confirm with the transport office before relying on an online transaction as the complete renewal.

Motorcycle and Scooter Renewal

Motorcycle and scooter owners should confirm:

  • the applicable province;
  • engine capacity (CC) - this is typically the primary classification factor;
  • private or commercial classification;
  • the current applicable provincial vehicle-tax schedule;
  • the renewal fee for the applicable category;
  • required insurance status;
  • pollution requirements where applicable;
  • renewal status (current, slightly overdue, or significantly overdue); and
  • any applicable late charges.

A 125cc motorcycle and a 200cc motorcycle do not automatically have the same renewal liability. A rate published for one province should not be applied to another.

Car and Jeep Renewal

Private car and jeep renewal costs depend on the applicable provincial vehicle-tax schedule and the vehicle's classification. Before payment, confirm:

  • provincial vehicle tax for the applicable vehicle category;
  • registration renewal charge;
  • required insurance;
  • pollution or inspection requirements where applicable;
  • late charges if applicable; and
  • any other prescribed fees.

Do not use a generic national car-tax figure when the applicable province uses a different schedule.

Electric Vehicle Renewal

Electric vehicles require particular attention because several different charges are frequently confused:

  • Annual vehicle tax - the recurring annual tax for a registered EV. This is determined by the applicable provincial schedule, typically based on motor power (kW) rather than engine capacity.
  • EV import taxation - a one-time charge when the vehicle enters Nepal. Includes customs duty and other applicable import charges. This is entirely separate from the annual renewal tax.
  • Registration and renewal - keeping the vehicle registration certificate valid, subject to the same general renewal framework as other vehicles.

A statement such as “EV tax in Nepal is Rs. X” is incomplete without specifying which charge, which province, which vehicle classification and which legal schedule applies.

Public and Commercial Vehicles

Public and commercial vehicles have additional regulatory requirements beyond private-vehicle renewal. Depending on the vehicle and service, these may include: route permits; passenger or carrying-capacity classifications; higher insurance requirements; additional inspection or certification; and other transport-management documents. The renewal framework for a private car does not describe the requirements for a public bus, taxi, truck or other commercial vehicle. Check the applicable transport authority for current requirements.

How to Estimate the Cost

The total amount payable during a renewal transaction is not a single nationwide figure. Think of it as:

Total = vehicle tax (provincial) + renewal fee + required insurance + applicable certification charges + late charges (if any) + other prescribed fees

Each component depends on the vehicle and applicable provincial rules. The government assessment or receipt is the final authority.

Before You Pay - Checklist

  • Identify the province and applicable provincial vehicle-tax schedule
  • Confirm vehicle category (bike, car, EV, commercial)
  • Confirm engine capacity (CC), motor power (kW) or other applicable specification
  • Check renewal status - current, recently expired, or significantly overdue
  • Confirm required insurance is valid
  • Check pollution certification requirements where applicable
  • Check route-permit requirements for commercial/public vehicles
  • Check for any current government notices, waivers or special procedures
  • Verify the total amount with the applicable government source before payment
  • Keep the government payment receipt and any digital confirmation

What Users Should Not Assume

Do not assume: A Bagmati rate applies nationwide

Bagmati is one province. Its tax schedule is province-specific.

Do not assume: An old blog table is the current rate

Provincial tax schedules can change through annual economic legislation.

Do not assume: Vehicle tax equals total renewal cost

Tax, renewal fee, insurance and other charges are separate.

Do not assume: Online tax payment means renewal is complete

Some steps may still require transport-office processing depending on the province.

Do not assume: Every province has the same digital service

Online availability varies and is still being rolled out in some provinces.

Do not assume: A long-overdue vehicle can be calculated as (tax × years)

Prolonged non-renewal can eventually involve cancellation and re-registration law, not ordinary late fees.

Do not assume: EV import tax equals EV annual road tax

These are completely different charges at different stages of vehicle ownership.

Do not assume: The same documents apply to every vehicle and province

Requirements vary by vehicle category and current office procedure.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Bluebook renewal in Nepal?

Bluebook renewal is the process of renewing a vehicle's registration certificate and completing applicable tax, fee, insurance and other requirements to keep the registration valid.

Is Bluebook renewal the same as vehicle tax?

No. Vehicle tax is a recurring provincial tax obligation. Renewal refers to the registration-certificate process. They can be handled in the same transaction but are legally separate.

Does vehicle tax differ by province in Nepal?

Yes. Each of Nepal's seven provinces administers vehicle taxation through its own applicable legal schedule.

How much does Bluebook renewal cost in Nepal?

There is no single nationwide amount. The total depends on the province, vehicle category, applicable tax, renewal fee, insurance, certification requirements and any late charges.

Can I pay vehicle tax online in Nepal?

In some provinces, yes. Online vehicle-tax and registration systems are operational or being rolled out through provincial government portals, but current availability varies.

Can I completely renew my Bluebook online?

Not always. Online tax payment does not automatically mean every step is complete. Some provinces may require additional office processing for registration to be fully renewed.

Is insurance required for Bluebook renewal?

The Motor Vehicles and Transport Management Act provides that required insurance must be in place and that registration renewal cannot proceed without it.

Is a pollution certificate required for renewal?

Where applicable rules require pollution certification, the relevant certificate must be available for renewal. This depends on vehicle type, age, fuel type and current procedure.

What happens if my Bluebook renewal is late?

Additional charges apply under both national registration-renewal law and potentially provincial tax-law provisions. Prolonged non-renewal can eventually lead to cancellation consequences under national law. These are two separate legal frameworks.

Can I renew my Bluebook for multiple years?

The Motor Vehicles and Transport Management Act provides a multi-year renewal provision for eligible vehicle categories, including private vehicles under applicable conditions, for up to five years. Confirm the current procedure with the responsible transport authority.

Is Bluebook renewal the same as driving licence renewal?

No. A Bluebook concerns vehicle registration. A driving licence concerns the driver. These are separate documents under the transport-management framework.

Is Bluebook renewal the same as route-permit renewal?

No. A route permit is a separate transport authorisation relevant to public or hired vehicles.

Who is the insurance regulator in Nepal?

The Nepal Insurance Authority (NIA) is the current regulatory body for vehicle insurance in Nepal and publishes applicable insurance circulars.

Where should I verify the final renewal amount?

Check the latest applicable provincial government schedule and the relevant Transport Management Office. A calculator provides an estimate; the government assessment or receipt is the final authority.

In Brief

Bluebook renewal is a vehicle-registration renewal process. The national vehicle law provides the registration framework, while provincial rules determine vehicle-tax obligations and many practical service arrangements. The exact amount depends on the vehicle, province and renewal status. Use the current government source for the final legal amount and service requirements, and use the NepaCalc Vehicle Tax Calculator when you need an estimate.
