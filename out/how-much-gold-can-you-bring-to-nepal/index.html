How Much Gold Can You Bring to Nepal?

Quick Answer

According to the Department of Customs, the duty-free allowance for gold jewellery is up to 25 grams for men and up to 50 grams for women. Nepali passengers may also bring up to 100 grams of raw gold by paying customs duty. These limits should always be checked against the latest official rules before travelling, as they can change.

How Much Gold Can You Bring to Nepal?

The amount of gold you can bring to Nepal depends on whether you are carrying gold jewellery or raw gold, and whether the gold falls within the duty-free allowance or requires customs duty. Gold Jewellery vs Raw Gold: What Is the Difference?

Nepal's customs rules treat gold jewellery and raw gold as separate categories, each with its own allowance and duty structure. Confusing the two categories is one of the most common errors travellers make at the border.

Gold jewellery refers to finished ornaments worn or carried as personal accessories. The duty-free allowance for gold jewellery is set at 25 grams for men and 50 grams for women, and additional jewellery beyond those limits may be brought in subject to customs duty.

Raw gold refers to unprocessed or semi-processed gold such as bars, coins, bullion, and other forms that are not finished jewellery. The duty-free allowance that applies to jewellery does not automatically apply to raw gold. If you know the weight of your items in international units, a gold converter can help translate that into grams for Nepali customs.

Important: Do not assume the jewellery allowance applies to gold bars, coins or other raw gold. The categories are treated separately by Nepal Customs.

Rules for Foreign Tourists Bringing Gold to Nepal

Foreign tourists visiting Nepal are subject to different customs provisions than Nepali passport holders returning from abroad. Tourists are generally permitted to bring personal gold jewellery for their own use as part of their baggage allowance, but the rules differ from the allowances that apply to returning Nepali citizens.

If you are a foreign tourist carrying gold into Nepal, you should declare it at customs if the value or quantity exceeds the applicable duty-free threshold. Carrying undeclared gold in excess of the permitted limit risks confiscation and penalties.

Confirm the exact allowance for foreign visitors directly with the Department of Customs before your journey, as the provisions for tourists are separate from those for Nepali nationals.

How Much Customs Duty Must You Pay on Gold in Nepal?

The customs duty payable on gold brought into Nepal depends on the applicable customs rules, the type and quantity of gold, and whether the gold falls within a passenger allowance.

How Is Gold Customs Duty Determined?

Before calculating customs duty, confirm:

  • Whether the gold is jewellery or raw gold.
  • Whether it falls within the applicable duty-free allowance.
  • Whether the passenger is eligible to bring the additional quantity under the current rules.
  • Which customs duty rate applies to that category of gold.

Do not assume that the same duty rate or allowance applies to jewellery, gold bars, and other forms of gold.

Check the Latest Official Customs Tariff

Nepal's Department of Customs publishes its integrated customs tariff, which is the official source to consult for applicable tariff rates. The exact customs duty rate is defined in Schedule 4 of the Customs Tariff Act, which can be verified via the Nepal Law Commission.

Important: Confirm the current rate with Nepal Customs before travelling. A rate quoted in an older article may no longer apply.

What Happens If You Bring More Gold Than Allowed?

Bringing gold into Nepal in excess of the permitted allowance without paying the applicable customs duty is a customs violation. The Department of Customs published passenger-goods notice sets out specific consequences for passengers who carry gold beyond the allowed limit.

Gold and gold jewellery brought in excess of the permitted limit and not declared at the customs checkpoint may be:

  • Confiscated by customs officials at the point of entry.
  • Subject to additional penalties under Nepal's customs law, including fines.
  • Treated as a customs offence in cases of deliberate concealment.

If you are carrying gold that may exceed the allowance, declare it at the customs desk on arrival. Paying the applicable duty is far less costly than risking confiscation or a penalty.

Practical Tips Before You Travel

  • Check the official rules before you travel. The Department of Customs and the passenger-goods notice are the authoritative sources. Read them directly rather than relying solely on third-party summaries.
  • Weigh your gold before your journey. Know the exact weight of any gold jewellery or raw gold you are carrying. Customs checks are conducted on weight, not estimated value alone.
  • Keep receipts and documentation. If you purchased gold abroad, carry the original receipt. This can support your declaration at the customs desk and may be required to confirm the nature and value of the item.
  • Declare voluntarily if in doubt. If you are uncertain whether your gold exceeds the allowance, declare it at the customs checkpoint. Voluntary declaration and payment of applicable duty is always the safest approach.
  • Calculate potential levies. If you are bringing raw gold or excess jewellery, you will need to pay customs duty. You can use a gold tax calculator to estimate your expenses in advance.
  • Do not mix categories. Do not carry raw gold and jewellery and assume they share a single combined allowance. Each category is assessed separately.
  • Check for annual updates. Nepal's customs tariff and passenger allowances are reviewed each fiscal year. What applied last year may not apply this year.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much gold can I bring to Nepal duty-free?

According to the Department of Customs, the duty-free gold jewellery allowance is up to 25 grams for men and up to 50 grams for women. These limits apply to gold jewellery, not raw gold. For broader context on market rules, read the Nepal gold price analysis for 2083.

Can I bring raw gold to Nepal from abroad?

According to the Department of Customs, Nepali passengers arriving from abroad may bring up to 100 grams of raw gold by paying customs duty. Do not assume the jewellery allowance applies to raw gold, bars or coins.

What customs duty do I pay on gold brought to Nepal?

The exact customs duty rate on gold in Nepal is set in Schedule 4 of the Customs Tariff Act. The applicable rate should be verified directly with the Department of Customs before travelling, as rates can change annually.

Does the jewellery allowance apply to gold bars and coins?

No. The duty-free jewellery allowance applies specifically to gold jewellery. Gold bars, coins and other forms of raw or semi-processed gold are assessed under a separate provision. Do not assume the same limit applies.

What happens if I bring more gold than allowed into Nepal?

Gold and gold jewellery brought in excess of the permitted allowance is subject to customs duty. If undeclared excess gold is detected at the border, it may be confiscated and the passenger may face additional penalties under customs law.

Do the gold allowance rules apply to foreign tourists?

Foreign tourists are subject to different customs provisions than returning Nepali nationals. Confirm the specific allowance with the Tribhuvan International Airport guidelines before your journey.
