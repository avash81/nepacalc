How Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) Is Calculated

Purchasing Power Parity calculates how many currency units are required to buy the exact same representative basket of goods and services (food, housing, healthcare, transportation) across borders.

World Bank ICP Conversion Formula:
Equivalent Salary (NP) = Base Salary (US) × [ PPP Factor (NP) ÷ PPP Factor (US) ]
Current calculation: $50,000 × [36.5 ÷ 1] = Rs.1,825,000 NPR

Frequently Asked Questions on PPP & International Salaries

What is Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) and how does it affect salaries?

Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) is an economic metric calculated by the World Bank and IMF that compares currencies through a standard 'basket of goods and services'. Because local prices for housing, groceries, healthcare, and education vary dramatically across nations, a $50,000 USD salary in the United States buys vastly more goods and services in India, Thailand, or Nepal than it does in New York or London. PPP adjusts salaries to match real buying power rather than misleading nominal exchange rates.

What is the Purchasing Power Parity between India and the United States?

According to the latest World Bank International Comparison Program (ICP) data, the PPP conversion factor for India is approximately 24.1 INR per 1 USD, whereas the market exchange rate is approximately 86.8 INR per 1 USD. This means a salary of $50,000 in the US provides the equivalent purchasing power of roughly ₹1,205,000 INR ($13,882 USD) in India. If a remote worker in India earns a full US salary of $50,000 (~₹4,340,000 INR), their local purchasing power is equivalent to earning over $180,000 in the United States (a 3.6× multiplier).

What is the World Bank PPP conversion factor formula?

The equivalent salary in the destination country (B) based on origin country (A) is calculated as: Equivalent Salary (B) = Base Salary (A) × [PPP Factor (B) ÷ PPP Factor (A)]. The World Bank computes these PPP conversion factors through the International Comparison Program (ICP) by tracking identical consumer baskets of hundreds of standardized products worldwide.

How should remote software engineers negotiate location-adjusted salaries?

When negotiating international or remote compensation, do not accept a 100% downward PPP adjustment. While local living costs may be 60–75% cheaper in developing economies, global products (laptops, phones, software subscriptions, international travel, imported cars) trade at global USD prices. Modern tech firms typically use a hybrid 50/50 model: 50% tied to the global benchmark rate and 50% adjusted to local living standards.

Does Dubai have personal income tax on salaries?

No. The United Arab Emirates (UAE) levies 0% personal income tax on employment salaries. In contrast, professionals in the UK or Germany face effective tax rates of 35% to 45%, and US professionals face federal and state taxes of 25% to 37%. Because of zero personal income tax, a $100,000 gross salary in Dubai yields higher net take-home pay than equivalent nominal salaries in London or Berlin.

What is the difference between Cost of Living Index and PPP?

The Cost of Living (CoL) index (such as Numbeo) measures consumer price levels relative to a reference city (typically New York City = 100) and includes heavy weighting on private rentals. PPP (Purchasing Power Parity), published by the World Bank, measures the macro-economic currency exchange rate that would equalize purchasing power across broad domestic GDP aggregates including public infrastructure, healthcare, and subsidized staples.
