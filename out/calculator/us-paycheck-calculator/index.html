How Is Your Paycheck Calculated? The Gross-to-Net Formula

A transparent breakdown of how gross salary converts to net take-home cash under IRS Publication 15-T and state revenue codes.

Take-Home Pay Formula: Gross Earnings - Pre-Tax Benefits - Federal Income Tax Withholding - FICA Taxes - State/Local Income Taxes - Post-Tax Deductions = Net Pay
Step 1: Gross Pay

Salary or Hourly Wages

Your base pay before any withholdings. For hourly employees, this includes regular hours plus overtime hours (FLSA 1.5x rate).

Step 2: Mandatory Taxes

Federal, FICA & State Taxes

Federal income tax (progressive 10% to 37%), FICA (6.2% Social Security up to $184,500 and 1.45% Medicare), and applicable state/local withholding.

Step 3: Net Cash

Take-Home Deposit

The actual net cash deposited into your checking account on payday after pre-tax benefits (401k, health) and voluntary deductions are subtracted.

Federal Income Tax Withholding vs. Actual Annual Tax Liability

One of the most common points of confusion for employees is the difference between paycheck withholding and annual tax liability.

Paycheck Withholding (IRS Publication 15-T)

The estimated amount an employer deducts from each paycheck based on your pay period frequency, wage brackets, and elections made on Form W-4. It is sent directly to the IRS throughout the tax year.

Actual Tax Liability (IRS Form 1040)

Your total final tax calculated when filing your federal tax return in April. It reconciles total income, outside investments, deductions, and tax credits. If withholding exceeded liability, you receive a refund; if insufficient, you owe a payment.

Calculation Methodology & Official References

NepaCalc computes employee payroll estimates strictly in accordance with published statutory federal and state tax guidelines:

  • Federal income tax withholding percentages and bracket steps follow the official percentage method outlined in IRS Publication 15-T (Federal Income Tax Withholding Methods).
  • To adjust allowances and credits on your tax return, refer to the Official IRS Form W-4 or test your year-end balance with the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator.
  • Social Security wage base limits are verified via the Social Security Administration (SSA) Contribution & Benefit Base ($184,500 for tax year 2026).
  • Overtime hours and minimum wage rules are grounded in the US Department of Labor Overtime Pay Requirements (FLSA 1.5×).
  • State disability contributions and state payroll withholding rates are derived from respective revenue authorities, such as the California Employment Development Department (EDD).

Frequently Asked Questions: US Paycheck & Take-Home Pay

How is net take-home pay calculated from gross salary?

Take-home pay starts with your gross pay (salary or hourly earnings). You subtract pre-tax deductions (like 401k or health insurance), federal income tax withholding (calculated via IRS Publication 15-T based on your W-4), FICA taxes (6.2% Social Security up to $184,500 and 1.45% Medicare), state/local income taxes, and any post-tax deductions (such as Roth 401k or garnishments). The remaining cash is your net paycheck.

What is the difference between bi-weekly and semi-monthly paychecks?

Bi-weekly employees are paid every two weeks, resulting in 26 paychecks per year (with two months having 3 paychecks). Semi-monthly employees are paid twice per month (typically on the 15th and last business day), resulting in 24 paychecks per year. Because of this, a semi-monthly paycheck is slightly larger than a bi-weekly paycheck for the exact same annual salary ($60,000 / 26 = $2,307.69 vs $60,000 / 24 = $2,500.00).

How do FICA payroll taxes work in 2026?

The Federal Insurance Contributions Act (FICA) funds Social Security and Medicare. In 2026, employees pay 6.2% in Social Security tax on earnings up to the official statutory wage base cap of $184,500 (maximum $11,439.00 per employee). Medicare tax is 1.45% on all earnings with no ceiling. An additional 0.9% Medicare surtax is withheld by employers when wages exceed $200,000, regardless of marital status.

Which US states have zero state income tax on paychecks?

There are nine states without a general individual state income tax on earned wages: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming. Living and working in these states means your state income tax withholding is $0.00, leaving only Federal income tax and FICA deductions.

How much is $17.50 an hour weekly and annually?

At $17.50 per hour working standard full-time hours (40 hours per week), gross earnings are $700.00 per week. Over a standard 52-week work year (2,080 hours), gross annual pay is $36,400. After federal taxes and FICA, estimated take-home pay is approximately $585 to $620 per week depending on state taxes and filing status.

What is $33.00 an hour annually?

At $33.00 per hour working 40 hours per week for 52 weeks (2,080 standard annual work hours), your gross annual salary is $68,640. In a state with no income tax like Texas (Single filer, standard deduction), estimated annual take-home pay is roughly $56,800, or about $2,185 bi-weekly.

What annual salary is $1,400 every two weeks?

If you receive $1,400 gross every two weeks, your gross annual salary is $36,400 ($1,400 x 26 pay periods). If $1,400 is your net take-home pay after taxes, your gross annual salary is approximately $43,000 to $48,000, depending on your state and tax elections.

If I make $1,000 a week, how much tax is taken out of my paycheck?

On a $1,000 weekly gross paycheck ($52,000/year), mandatory taxes typically take 18% to 25% ($180 to $250 per check). This includes Social Security ($62.00 at 6.2%), Medicare ($14.50 at 1.45%), federal income tax withholding (~$75 to $95), and state income tax ($0 in 0% states up to $45 in progressive tax states), leaving an estimated net check of $750 to $820.

What is the difference between paycheck withholding and my actual tax liability?

Paycheck withholding is an estimate withheld each pay period by your employer under IRS Publication 15-T rules. Your actual tax liability is calculated once a year when filing IRS Form 1040 in April. If your total withholding exceeds your actual tax liability, you receive a tax refund; if withholding was insufficient, you owe taxes.

How does contributing to a 401(k) or HSA increase take-home value?

Traditional 401(k) contributions reduce your gross taxable income for Federal and State income taxes, lowering your immediate tax bill. Section 125 health insurance premiums and HSA contributions are triple-tax-advantaged, reducing Federal income tax, State income tax, and FICA payroll taxes simultaneously in most states.
