Gold-Silver Ratio Today

81.6oz of silver = 1 oz of gold

The gold-silver ratio shows how many ounces of silver it takes to buy one ounce of gold. A ratio above 80 is historically high and often interpreted as silver being undervalued relative to gold. The ratio has ranged from ~15:1 (early 20th century) to over 120:1 (2020 pandemic peak). Long-term precious metals investors use this ratio to decide when to rotate between gold and silver holdings.

What Is COMEX? How Is Spot Price Set?

COMEX (Commodity Exchange, a CME Group division, New York) is the world's primary marketplace for gold and silver futures. The global spot price is derived from the most active front-month COMEX futures contract in combination with the London OTC market — this is what all online live price feeds stream in real time. Physical gold at US coin shops trades at “spot plus a dealer premium” — roughly $30–$80/oz for gold coins and $3–$8/oz for silver coins — to cover minting, distribution, and dealer margin.

What is the gold price today in the USA?

What is the gold-silver ratio today?

The gold-silver ratio today is approximately 81.6:1, meaning it takes about 81.6 ounces of silver to equal the value of 1 ounce of gold. Historically the ratio has ranged from 15:1 to over 120:1. Investors use it as a relative valuation tool — a high ratio (above 80) generally suggests silver is undervalued relative to gold, while a low ratio (below 60) suggests the opposite.

What is COMEX and how does it set the gold spot price?

COMEX (Commodity Exchange) is a division of the CME Group based in New York and is the world's primary exchange for gold and silver futures and options. The global spot price for gold is largely derived from the most active front-month COMEX futures contract, combined with the London OTC market. This continuous price discovery is what all major online spot price trackers — including this page — stream in real time.

What is the difference between spot price and what I pay at a coin shop?

The spot price is the wholesale benchmark for immediate delivery of institutional-grade bullion (typically 400 oz London Good Delivery bars). When buying physical products like American Gold Eagle coins or silver rounds at a US coin shop, you pay a 'dealer premium' above spot — typically $30–$80/oz for gold coins and $3–$8/oz for silver coins — to cover manufacturing, minting, distribution, and dealer margin.

How often does the gold spot price change?

The gold spot price changes continuously, virtually 24 hours a day, 5 days a week, as global markets in Sydney, Tokyo, London, and New York trade in sequence. Prices are quoted in real time and can move by several dollars per ounce within seconds based on economic data releases, Federal Reserve announcements, geopolitical events, or large institutional trades.

Is gold a good investment in the USA right now?

Gold is widely regarded as a safe-haven asset and inflation hedge. Its investment merits depend on your portfolio goals, time horizon, and risk tolerance. Gold typically performs well during periods of high inflation, geopolitical uncertainty, or US dollar weakness. However, gold does not generate income (no dividends or interest), so it is primarily used for portfolio diversification and wealth preservation rather than growth.

What is the weight difference between a Troy Ounce and a standard Ounce?

Precious metals are always measured in Troy Ounces (oz t), not standard avoirdupois ounces. 1 Troy Ounce = 31.1035 grams, while 1 standard ounce = 28.3495 grams. This means a Troy Ounce is about 10% heavier than a regular ounce. All gold and silver spot prices worldwide are quoted per Troy Ounce.

What are the most common gold karats in the USA?

In the United States, 14 Karat (58.3% pure) is the most popular karat for jewelry due to its durability and appearance. 10 Karat (41.7% pure) is the legal minimum to be sold as real gold under FTC guidelines. For investment bullion, 24 Karat (99.9% pure — American Gold Buffalo coins) and 22 Karat (91.67% pure — American Gold Eagle coins) are the standard. 18K is common in higher-end jewelry.

What is the difference between COMEX and LBMA gold prices?

COMEX (New York) provides continuous real-time price discovery from futures trading that powers most live spot price feeds. The LBMA (London Bullion Market Association) publishes official twice-daily benchmark fixings — the LBMA Gold Price AM and PM — used by central banks, miners, and long-term physical supply contracts. Most retail spot price tools display the continuous COMEX-derived rate, while institutional physical contracts are priced against LBMA daily fixings.

Why is silver more volatile than gold?

Silver is both a precious metal and a critical industrial commodity used heavily in solar panels, electronics, electric vehicles, and medical devices. Its total market size is much smaller than gold's. As a result, significant buying or selling volume causes larger percentage price swings in silver than in gold. During economic recessions, industrial demand falls sharply, hitting silver harder. During economic expansions or green-energy booms, silver often outperforms gold significantly.
